Distribution-Aware Companding Quantization in LLMs
Investigated whether modeling transformer weight distributions improves LLM quantization. Systematically showed that weights in Llama3-8B and Qwen2.5-7B follow a logistic distribution (best fit in 109/192 and 111/168 submodules respectively), then built DACQ — a PTQ framework applying per-layer CDF companding before 4-bit quantization. DACQ achieves lower weight reconstruction MSE than AWQ, yet perplexity does not improve — surfacing a key finding: outlier weights are statistically rare but task-critical, and distribution-aware compression penalizes them. The gap between reconstruction fidelity and downstream quality is itself the contribution.
Lower MSE than AWQ · 19% throughput gain over FP16 · arXiv:2603.00364 · Feb 2026

Edit-REPA: Representation-Aligned Diffusion for Image Editing
Identified a representation bottleneck in instruction-based diffusion editors: early timesteps operate on heavily corrupted latents, causing semantic drift and structural collapse before the edit is applied. Proposed Edit-REPA, which anchors U-Net mid-block features to frozen pretrained encoders (DINOv2 for structure, SigLIP/CLIP for semantics) during fine-tuning of InstructPix2Pix. Training converged stably with cosine alignment plateauing at ~0.8, and qualitative results show reduced drift and better global structure preservation vs. the unaligned baseline. Extended the framework design to a PixArt-α DiT editor and Conditional Flow Matching objectives.
Stable alignment (cosine sim ~0.8) · Qualitative drift reduction · Novel application of REPA to editing

Optimizing Transformer-based Sequential Recommender Systems
Studied the effect of system-level optimizations on training efficiency of BERT4Rec scaled from 5M to 430M parameters, across NVIDIA A100, V100, and L4 GPUs. Focus was on iLoRA (instance-wise LoRA) and mixed-precision training: AMP alone reduced peak memory from 11.2 GB to 7.2 GB on the 130M model. On the 430M model, iLoRA+AMP+torch.compile (reduce-overhead) cut step time from 470ms to 130ms and memory from 10.4 GB to 2.85 GB — while using only 0.96% of trainable parameters and retaining recommendation accuracy.
72% step time reduction (iLoRA) · 70% memory reduction · 99% fewer trainable params

Adversarial Attacks on ImageNet Models
Implemented FGSM, PGD, and patch-based adversarial attacks on a pretrained ResNet-34 (ImageNet baseline: 76% Top-1), all constrained to ε=0.02 pixel-space perturbation. FGSM dropped Top-1 to 3.4% in a single gradient step; PGD (20 iterations, α=0.005) achieved complete model failure at 0% Top-1 with an 87.4% attack success rate. A localized patch attack (64×64, ε=0.5, 50 iterations) achieved 91% success rate. Also evaluated cross-architecture transferability to DenseNet-121 — PGD transferred most effectively (45.5%), while patch attacks transferred poorly (25.5%), exposing the role of architecture-specific spatial representations in adversarial robustness.
PGD: 76% → 0% Top-1 · 91% patch success · 45.5% cross-arch transfer rate
